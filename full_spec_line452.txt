## 4.1 Group Discussion Rubric

### Criterion 1: Participation

**4 — Excellent:** Actively and consistently engages with the discussion, providing valuable insights.

**3 — Good:** Regularly contributes with thoughtful ideas, though may lack depth.

**2 — Average:** Contributes occasionally with relevant ideas but lacks consistency.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Rarely contributes or engages with the discussion.

### Criterion 2: Listening Skills

**4 — Excellent:** Demonstrates exceptional listening skills, respects others' ideas, and supports group dynamics.

**3 — Good:** Actively listens and avoids interrupting, showing empathy.

**2 — Average:** Generally attentive but occasionally interrupts or loses focus.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Frequently interrupts or demonstrates poor listening skills.

### Criterion 3: Content Relevance

**4 — Excellent:** Contributions are highly relevant, insightful, and add significant value to the discussion.

**3 — Good:** Contributions are relevant and well-thought-out but lack depth.

**2 — Average:** Contributions are moderately relevant but lack focus or clarity.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Contributions are irrelevant or fail to align with the discussion topic.

### Criterion 4: Collaboration

**4 — Excellent:** Works exceptionally well with the group, fostering a positive and productive dynamic.

**3 — Good:** Collaborates effectively, resolving conflicts constructively.

**2 — Average:** Collaborates inconsistently, with occasional conflicts.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Does not collaborate effectively; may disrupt group dynamics.

### Criterion 5: Problem Solving

**4 — Excellent:** Consistently contributes innovative and effective solutions to group challenges.

**3 — Good:** Actively engages in problem-solving with creative solutions.

**2 — Average:** Provides basic solutions but lacks depth or creativity.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Rarely contributes to problem-solving or offers ineffective solutions.

## 4.2 Free Writing Rubric

### Criterion 1: Relevance

**4 — Excellent:** Responses are highly relevant, directly addressing the questions with clarity and precision.

**3 — Good:** Responses are relevant and address the questions effectively but with minor gaps.

**2 — Average:** Responses are moderately relevant but lack focus or depth.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Responses are irrelevant or fail to address the questions.

### Criterion 2: Understanding

**4 — Excellent:** Demonstrates a deep understanding of concepts with accurate and insightful answers.

**3 — Good:** Demonstrates good understanding with mostly accurate responses.

**2 — Average:** Demonstrates basic understanding but contains minor inaccuracies.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Demonstrates limited understanding with superficial or partially correct responses.

### Criterion 3: Critical Thinking

**4 — Excellent:** Provides strong analysis and logical reasoning, offering well-supported arguments or interpretations.

**3 — Good:** Demonstrates thoughtful analysis with some logical reasoning but lacks depth.

**2 — Average:** Provides basic analysis, with minimal reasoning or support.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Lacks analysis or reasoning; answers are superficial or unsupported.

### Criterion 4: Clarity

**4 — Excellent:** Writing is exceptionally clear, concise, and well-structured.

**3 — Good:** Writing is clear and organized, with minor lapses in flow or clarity.

**2 — Average:** Writing is somewhat clear but lacks strong organization or detail.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Writing is unclear, disorganized, or difficult to understand.

### Criterion 5: Completeness

**4 — Excellent:** Fully addresses all parts of the question, providing detailed and comprehensive answers.

**3 — Good:** Answers most parts with minor omissions.

**2 — Average:** Answers some parts but several sections are incomplete.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Major gaps; many parts of the question are unanswered.

## 4.3 Reflective Journal Rubric

### Criterion 1: Experience (Concrete Experience)

**4 — Excellent:** Clearly and accurately describes all learning activities (theory, lab, self-study); shows strong engagement and observation.

**3 — Good:** Describes most learning activities clearly with minor missing details.

**2 — Average:** Describes activities but lacks clarity or important details.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Very limited or unclear description of activities.

### Criterion 2: Reflection (Feelings & Learning Experience)

**3 — Good:** Good reflection with some insight into learning experience.

**2 — Average:** Basic reflection with limited depth or personal insight.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Superficial reflection with little personal connection.

### Criterion 3: Analysis (Conceptual Understanding)

**4 — Excellent:** Shows deep conceptual understanding with strong links between theory and experience; insights are accurate and meaningful.

**3 — Good:** Shows good conceptual understanding with correct connections.

**2 — Average:** Shows basic understanding with minor inaccuracies.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Shows weak understanding with unclear or incorrect concepts.

### Criterion 4: Action Plan (Application & Improvement)

**4 — Excellent:** Clear, realistic, and detailed plan for applying learning and improving performance.

**2 — Average:** Basic plan mentioned with limited detail.

**1 — Needs Improvement:** Vague or unclear plan with no real direction.

**4 — Excellent:** Writing is very clear, well-organized, reflective in tone, and easy to understand.

**3 — Good:** Writing is clear with minor errors in flow or structure.

# 8. PYTHON VALIDATION AND FINAL SCORE CALCULATION

Python must be the authoritative scoring and validation layer.

Do not allow the language model to independently decide or calculate the final published marks.

Store these separately:

- `faculty_total_marks`
- `ai_total_marks`
- `ai_criterion_scores`
- `final_total_marks`
- `discrepancy_value`
- `discrepancy_threshold`
- `calculation_method`

## 8.1 Discrepancy calculation

Let:

- F = Faculty overall marks.
- A = AI overall marks.
- M = Maximum assessment marks.
- D = Absolute difference between faculty and AI marks.

Calculate:

`D = abs(F - A)`

Default discrepancy threshold:

`T = 0.20 × M`

The 20% threshold must be configurable by Admin, with 20% as the initial default.

## 8.2 Final score policy

Apply the following exact rules:

**Case 1: Difference is greater than the threshold**

If:

`D > T`

Then:

`Final Marks = (F + A) / 2`

**Case 2: Difference is equal to or less than the threshold**

If:

`D <= T`

Then:

`Final Marks = F`

Important:
- Exactly equal to the threshold must NOT trigger averaging.
- Do not use `>=` for the averaging condition.
- Preserve both original evaluation scores.
- Allow decimal or half-mark precision.
- Do not round in a way that changes the scoring policy.
- Validate that the final score is within the assessment maximum.

### Example 1

Maximum marks = 20

Faculty marks = 15

AI marks = 10

Difference = 5

Threshold = 4

Since 5 > 4:

Final marks = (15 + 10) / 2 = 12.5

### Example 2

Maximum marks = 20

Faculty marks = 15

AI marks = 11

Difference = 4

Threshold = 4

Since 4 is NOT greater than 4:

Final marks = 15

The faculty score is retained.

Admin must be able to configure the discrepancy threshold for future assessments. Preserve the threshold used at the time of each calculation for audit purposes.
